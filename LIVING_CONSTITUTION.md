# The Living Constitution

**Status:** Proposed companion charter (v0.1.0)  
**Applies to:** This repository's constitution and any projects that voluntarily adopt it  
**Related documents:** [CONSTITUTION.md](CONSTITUTION.md) · [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)

## Preamble

The Freelance Developer Constitution is a **living, adaptable framework** for human–AI partnership in software development. It is not a final answer, a permanent rulebook, or a demand that every creator follow the same methods.

We start from an enduring purpose: **give people—especially working people, students, small businesses, and creators with limited financial resources—the ability to build, understand, own, change, export, and deploy their software without unnecessary gatekeeping or lock-in.**

As tools, community needs, and knowledge change, our framework should improve too.

> **No one should need permission from a single company, author, AI system, or committee to develop an independent interpretation of these ideas, once the material is made available under a license permitting adaptation.**

## Article 1 — A foundation, not a ceiling

1. Treat the constitution as a starting point for learning, experimentation, critical thinking, and collaboration.
2. Encourage improvements, corrections, translations, accessible formats, alternative workflows, and new articles.
3. Support different project circumstances: local-first, self-hosted, commercial, nonprofit, educational, or hybrid.
4. Keep the underlying objectives visible: freedom, accessibility, human control, honest verification, privacy, security, and the ability to leave a platform.

## Article 2 — Independent adaptation and forks

We intend for individuals and communities to be able to:

- Copy and adapt constitutional wording for their own projects.
- Extend or simplify articles, add examples, and document their own governance.
- Maintain separate versions when they disagree with changes in the upstream repository.
- Link back to the original and explain significant differences without presenting a fork as the official upstream text.
- Suggest improvements upstream without being required to surrender control of their independent work.

**Legal clarification:** A public repository alone does not grant copyright permissions to copy, adapt, or redistribute its text. This project has not yet selected a license for its documentation. These freedoms describe the *intended model*; a suitable explicit content license, such as CC BY 4.0, must be added to make the permission legally clear. Until then, ask the relevant rights holders before redistributing adapted versions. A license for the constitution's text is separate from the licenses for any software built under it.

## Article 3 — Humans and AI can both improve the framework

Humans may identify problems, propose new principles, change their minds, evaluate consequences, and decide what to adopt.

AI tools may research alternatives, identify contradictions, draft amendments, compare versions, generate supporting examples, and assist with reviews. Their outputs are proposals, not authority. Contributors should disclose meaningful AI assistance when submitting material.

No AI-generated suggestion is considered accepted, verified, or released merely because it was generated. Authorized human maintainers evaluate and approve upstream changes.

## Article 4 — Shared upstream governance versus independent governance

The **upstream repository** needs review and coordination to avoid contradictory or accidental edits. Human maintainers decide whether to merge submissions into that shared edition, publish their reasons for major changes, and maintain a public change history.

That decision-making power applies **only to the upstream version**. It is not ownership of every adaptation or control over unrelated forks. Maintainers should not portray their version as the only legitimate interpretation.

Independent projects may select their own governance structures, while remaining bound by the terms of any licenses that apply.

## Article 5 — A transparent amendment process

1. **Identify the issue.** Describe what is missing, outdated, inaccessible, harmful, impractical, or contradictory.
2. **Propose a change.** Use a GitHub issue or pull request with the text, rationale, and affected articles.
3. **Assess consequences.** Explain the impact on human agency, affordability, creator ownership, portability, privacy, security, and collaboration.
4. **Discuss openly.** Invite respectful review and alternatives. Give material reductions in rights or access extra scrutiny and time for comment.
5. **Decide transparently.** Maintainers record acceptance, rejection, or deferral with reasons; disagreements may remain visible.
6. **Version and preserve.** Update the change history and version; preserve earlier revisions in Git history and, for major releases, tags.
7. **Revisit.** Amendments may themselves be challenged and amended later.

Use a patch version for editorial clarification, a minor version for compatible new principles, and a major version for substantial shifts in scope or commitments. This is guidance, not a claim that v1.0 has been adopted.

## Article 6 — Freedom includes freedom to disagree

Good-faith disagreement is welcome. A contributor may advocate a different design, choose to use another AI provider, or create a separate edition of the constitution.

Feedback should address claims and consequences rather than attacking people. Dissent does not entitle anyone to merge changes into a shared upstream repository; maintainers must still use consistent, publicly explained review criteria.

## Article 7 — No retroactive removal of rights

Once material is distributed under a qualifying open license, a future change to the upstream text or license cannot retroactively cancel the rights already granted under that license. Future editions may differ, subject to legal obligations.

A constitution cannot itself override applicable laws, third-party license restrictions, privacy rights, or the independent consent of contributors.

## Article 8 — The test of continued evolution

A revision should make this framework more understandable, adoptable, and useful. Before accepting a major change, ask:

- Does it expand people's ability to create without unnecessary financial barriers?
- Can creators still understand, own, modify, export, and independently operate their work?
- Are AI systems serving human judgment rather than claiming authority over it?
- Have implementation costs, risks, and tradeoffs been explained honestly?
- Can other groups continue developing their own versions independently?

If the answer is uncertain, document the disagreement and make the smallest responsible change.

---

**This document is a proposed companion to the [core constitution](CONSTITUTION.md).** It clarifies that the constitution is meant to evolve and that upstream stewardship must not turn into central control over independent editions.

**Guiding principle:** *We build a foundation that future creators are free to question and improve—including the parts we wrote ourselves.*
